    Materials: cards, parameters and what a card does¶

    A softsim material is a card: one YAML file (or mapping) that holds everything about a soft material that a simulation needs, plus where the numbers come from and how far they can be trusted. Every number on a card can be changed the same way as any other config value.

    A card¶

    name: my_silicone
description: What it is.
source: Where every number comes from. Say "uncalibrated" or "fitted to X" honestly.
kind: physical                # physical | effective (see below)
density: 1070 kg/m^3
elastic:
  model: yeoh                 # linear | stvk | neo_hookean | stable_neo_hookean | mooney_rivlin | yeoh
  c10: 12 kPa                 #   | ogden | gent | arruda_boyce | magneto_active
  c20: 0.8 kPa
  poisson_ratio: 0.49         # or bulk_modulus: 1.2 MPa
damping:                      # optional
  model: prony                # prony (a material model) | rayleigh | kelvin_voigt (numerical damping)
  g: [0.5, 0.2]
  tau: [2 ms, 50 ms]
friction: {sliding: 1.0}
adhesion: {strength: 14 kPa, range: 0.3 mm}   # optional tack (cohesive law)
validity:                     # where the data behind the card was taken
  strain: [-0.5, 3.0]         # nominal uniaxial strain range
  strain_rate: [0 1/s, 300 1/s]
  temperature: 23 degC
uncertainty:                  # one standard deviation (normal, lognormal) or half-width (uniform)
  elastic.c10: {rel: 0.08}
  damping.g[0]: {abs: 0.05, dist: uniform}
references:
  - {doi: 10.1089/soro.2019.0115, note: uniaxial tension data}

    Elastic laws. All laws accept unit strings ("12 kPa"). Their parameters:

    model parameters notes
    linear, stvk, neo_hookean, stable_neo_hookean any two of youngs_modulus, poisson_ratio, shear_modulus, bulk_modulus stored as E and ν
    mooney_rivlin c10, c01
    yeoh c10, c20, c30 often the best simple fit for silicones
    ogden mu: [...], alpha: [...] N terms
    gent mu, jm stiffens without bound as I1 − 3 → J_m
    arruda_boyce mu, lambda_m five-term series (Abaqus form); locking stretch λ_m
    magneto_active matrix (any hyperelastic law above), susceptibility or particle_volume_fraction, along, normal, demagnetization iron-filled (MRE): soft-magnetic in the scene's magnetic_field; reference backend only (magnetic.md)

    The laws with a bulk modulus take bulk_modulus or poisson_ratio. softsim material show <card> prints the small-strain equivalents (E0, μ0, κ, ν0), wave speeds and explicit time-step estimates of any card.

    Rate dependence. prony is a material model: generalised Maxwell relaxation of the shear (isochoric) part, G(t) = G0 (1 − Σ g_i (1 − e^(−t/τ_i))). The elastic constants are the instantaneous ones, so the long-term shear modulus is G0 (1 − Σ g). rayleigh and kelvin_voigt are numerical damping: their loss grows without bound with frequency. They help stabilise or match one experiment, but they are not a description of a silicone.

    Prony runs on every solver with the same update and energies:

    • the reference FEM and the homogeneous tests;
    • MuJoCo and SOFA. There the backend's own elasticity carries the long-term law, and the decaying Maxwell overstresses are added as vertex forces each step (softsim/materials/viscoelastic.py). These forces are explicit, so softsim check verifies that the time step resolves them.

    Prony needs a law with an isochoric/volumetric split, so not linear or stvk.

    Physical and effective cards. kind: physical means that the numbers describe the material and should hold on any solver that represents the law well. kind: effective means the numbers reproduce data only with one backend, mesh and contact model. Calibrating a backend against an experiment produces such cards (the *_drop_fit* presets). An effective card must name backend and mesh_size in its provenance.

    The library¶

    softsim materials lists every card.

    • Preferred physical cards: the *_roels2025 cards for 7 silicones (Ecoflex 00-20/00-30/00-50, Dragon Skin 20/30, Mold Max 14NV, Mold Star 30). They are fitted to tension and compression specimens together (Roels et al. 2025, CC-BY-4.0), with a measured slow Prony series (1 s to 3 h). Ecoflex 00-30 and 00-50 also carry measured fast relaxation for impacts (see below).
    • Older physical cards: the *_marechal2021 cards for 18 silicones (Marechal et al. 2021), from tension only. Their moduli are probably ~3× too low, because the strain was measured at the crosshead.
    • All are listed with their fit quality and caveats in material_library.md.
    • Datasheet estimates: ecoflex_00_10, ecoflex_00_30, dragon_skin_10.
    • Effective drop-test fits: ecoflex_00_10_drop_fit*.

    To fit your own data, write a kind: material_tests dataset file and run the commands below. See datasets/roels2025_elastomers.yaml for several test kinds and specimens per material, or datasets/marechal2021_srmd.yaml for one curve.

    softsim fetch datasets/marechal2021_srmd.yaml                     # downloads + checks the archive
softsim fit-material datasets/marechal2021_srmd.yaml ecoflex_00_30 --strain-max 1.0
# every law: cards with uncertainty, report.yaml, fit.png in runs/material_fits/...
softsim material project ecoflex_00_30_marechal2021 --to neo_hookean --strain-range=-0.3,0.6 --out eco30_nh.yaml

    fit-material fits neo_hookean, mooney_rivlin, yeoh, ogden1, ogden2, gent and arruda_boyce. It ranks them by AIC, reports parameter uncertainties, and flags laws that lose stability (stress decreasing with stretch) in uniaxial, equibiaxial or planar loading within the fitted range.

    project fits another law to a card's own response, for backends that lack the card's law. MuJoCo has only stable Neo-Hookean, and its refusal message points to this command.

    Fast rate dependence (impacts)¶

    Tension tests run at about 0.4/s, while an impact loads the material within milliseconds. Silicones are much stiffer and lossier there. Ecoflex 00-30, for example, is 1.65× stiffer at 100 Hz and 2.2× at 1 kHz than in its tension test. A card without fast-rate data is therefore too soft and too bouncy in impacts.

    If you have a frequency-domain fit (DMA, rheometer, guided waves) as a fractional Kelvin–Voigt model μ0 (1 + (iωτ)ⁿ), convert it to Prony branches and add them to a card. The response at the test rate stays as fitted:

    from softsim.materials import load_material, save_material
from softsim.materials.viscoelastic import prony_from_fractional, with_fast_relaxation

a, taus, rms = prony_from_fractional(n=0.20, tau=2.9e-3, band=(1.0, 2000.0))   # Hz
card = with_fast_relaxation(load_material("my_silicone"), a, taus)
save_material(card, "my_silicone_rate.yaml")
    • Band: branches are placed only inside the band. Keep its top below 1/(2π·dt) of the backend you run, and its bottom where your measured relaxation starts, so nothing is counted twice.
    • Source of the library's values: Croquette et al. 2026 (arXiv:2604.27722), who measured Ecoflex 00-30 and 00-50 with guided elastic waves and checked them against a rheometer from 1 to 100 Hz.
    • Assumption: only the shape of their curve relative to the test rate is used, applied to the isochoric part at large strain. Their samples were about 2× softer than Roels', so their static modulus is not used.

    Changing values¶

    Every value is a dotted key, the same everywhere:

    softsim params scenarios/drop_test/cases/w050.yaml --grep material   # every key, value, unit, allowed range
softsim run scenarios/drop_test/cases/w050.yaml \
    --set specimen.material.elastic.youngs_modulus='40 kPa' \
    --set specimen.material.damping.model=prony \
    --set 'specimen.material.damping.g=[0.6]' --set 'specimen.material.damping.tau=[2 ms]'
softsim material show ecoflex_00_10 --set elastic.model=gent --set elastic.mu='30 kPa' --set elastic.jm=20
    • Switching a law: setting elastic.model (or damping.model) keeps only the parameters the new law understands. Going from an E/ν law to one with a bulk modulus carries ν over.
    • List entries are indexed: elastic.mu[1], damping.tau[0].
    • Sweeps: the same keys work in sweep grids (softsim sweep).
    • Which values matter: softsim sensitivity runs a Morris screening over value ranges and reports each parameter's influence (μ*) and non-linearity/interaction (σ) on any summary metric: 
      softsim sensitivity scenarios/drop_test/cases/w050.yaml --metric peak_indentation --jobs 8 \
    --param "specimen.material.elastic.youngs_modulus=30 kPa:150 kPa" \
    --param "specimen.material.damping.beta=0.1 ms:3 ms" --param "drop.height=0.2:0.4"
    • Uncertainty propagation: softsim sweep sweep.yaml --sample 32 --seed 0 (or a sample: block in the sweep file) crosses the grid with 32 variants of the material, drawn from its card's uncertainty. The drawn values become sample.<key> columns of results.parquet.
    • Python: 
      from softsim.materials import load_material, sample
m = load_material("ecoflex_00_10", overrides=["elastic.youngs_modulus=40 kPa"])
m2 = m.with_overrides(["elastic.model=yeoh", "elastic.c10=12 kPa"])
variants = sample(card_with_uncertainty, 64, seed=0)  # draws from `uncertainty`
    • At run time:
    • Simulation.set_material(body, material, overrides) rebuilds the model;
    • set_material(body, None, overrides) changes the current material;
    • ROS: the set_material service takes the same overrides list.

    What a card does: homogeneous tests¶

    softsim.materials.homogeneous computes the exact response of a card in the standard laboratory tests, without a mesh or a backend. It needs the fem extra (JAX). The energies and the Prony update are those of the reference FEM.

    from softsim.materials.homogeneous import curve, relaxation, constant_rate, dma
curve(m, "uniaxial", [0.5, 1.5, 2.0])        # also equibiaxial, planar, simple_shear, volumetric, confined
curve(m, "uniaxial", [1.5], "instantaneous") # infinitely fast; default "relaxed" = equilibrium
relaxation(m, strain=0.05)                   # ramp-and-hold
constant_rate(m, "uniaxial", 2.0, rate=100)  # strain rate in 1/s
dma(m, [1, 10, 100])                         # storage and loss modulus vs frequency

    softsim material plot ecoflex_00_10 my_silicone.yaml --tests uniaxial,simple_shear,relaxation,dma --out cards.png

    These are checked against closed forms for every law (tests/test_homogeneous.py):

    • uniaxial, biaxial and planar stresses;
    • small-strain moduli;
    • Prony relaxation;
    • DMA against the analytic G*(ω).

    From a physical card to a backend (transfer)¶

    A physical card holds on any solver that represents its law well. The tetrahedral backends don't fully do so: linear elements lock, and MuJoCo's contact loses energy. To find the parameters with which a backend reproduces a physical card in a given scenario:

    1. Run the card on the locking-free reference with the scenario's conditions, and save the runs as tracks. tools/make_reference_tracks.py does this for the drop test with each recording's measured impact (softsim.experiments.reference_tracks).
    2. Calibrate the backend against those tracks. This is an ordinary softsim calibrate (e.g. calibrations/transfer_ecoflex_00_10_mujoco.yaml). The result is the backend's effective card, and the remaining NRMSE is its error bar against the physical card.

    For Ecoflex 00-10 in the drop test, the physical values are 143 kPa, 0.69 and 2 ms. With a 5 mm mesh:

    • MuJoCo reproduces the card within 3–10% using E0 = 66 kPa, g = 0.94, τ = 5.5 ms;
    • SOFA within 3–7% using 79.6 kPa, 0.77 and 2.7 ms (experiments.md).

    A transferred card is only valid for loads like the ones it was transferred on. The MuJoCo card above (fitted on 20 and 50 g) relaxes 94% within a few milliseconds. Under 500 g it crushes the specimen until MuJoCo's tetrahedra invert, and the run stops with "simulation diverged".

    Joint fits: lab tests and dynamic data together¶

    Lab tests fix the quasi-static stiffness. An impact sees the instantaneous one and how fast it relaxes. Neither alone pins down a viscoelastic card:

    • tension data can't tell how much of the stiffness relaxes within milliseconds;
    • the drop data can't tell what the material does a second later.

    A calibration file can therefore include material-test terms next to its simulated cases (softsim.experiments.calibrate). They are evaluated on the card's homogeneous-test curves, take milliseconds, and add to the same cost:

    cases: {w020: 1.0, w050: 1.0}
material_tests:
  # specimens from a dataset, one term per test kind and strain window
  - {dataset: ../datasets/roels2025_elastomers.yaml, material: ecoflex_00_30, kind: tension, strain: [0, 1.0]}
  # relaxation: the normalised hold, so it tests rate dependence only
  - {dataset: ../datasets/roels2025_elastomers.yaml, material: ecoflex_00_30, kind: relaxation}
  # or inline points: a datasheet's "100% modulus" is often all there is
  - {name: datasheet_100pct, points: {strain: [1.0], stress: [55 kPa]}, weight: 0.5}

    A term's cost is its RMSE relative to the largest stress, averaged over specimens. The term is compared with the card's fully relaxed curve, unless it has a test rate: the dataset's strain_rate, or rate: in the entry. With a rate, a Prony card is loaded at that rate.

    A relaxation term ramps the card to each recording's hold strain at rate (default 0.4/s) and holds it. Both curves are divided by their value at the start of the recorded hold, which begins hold_offset (default 1.3 s) after the ramp, as in Roels et al. 2025. Ecoflex 00-30's library card matches its 3-hour hold within 0.35%; without damping it is off by 1.4%.

    Example: Ecoflex 00-10. The drop-only fit on the 3D reference (E0 ≈ 150 kPa, g ≈ 0.7) relaxes to E∞ ≈ 44 kPa. The datasheet's 100% modulus (55 kPa) implies about 94 kPa. Every point that drop-only fit has evaluated misses the datasheet by 40–60%. calibrations/ecoflex_00_10_drop_fem_joint.yaml fits both together. After 80 evaluations: E0 = 190 kPa, g = 0.60, τ = 0.58 ms, E∞ = 76 kPa, the datasheet term 20% off (from 40–60%), the 20 g first contact 14% and the 50 g one 3% (experiments.md).
