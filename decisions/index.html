    Open decisions¶

    Each entry: the question, the options, what I recommend and why, and what I did meanwhile so nothing waits on it. Answer by editing the Decision line (or tell me in the chat); I will then do what the entry says.

    1. MuJoCo timing fix: keep or revert¶

    Since the prescribed-motion work, MuJoCo's step is split mj_step1 → explicit forces → mj_step2, and state() recomputes kinematics, because after mj_step MuJoCo's positions are one step stale (the forces and the reported state were a step old). It moves documented MuJoCo numbers by 1.5–3% (nrmse) and one 0.1 ms step in the time of a peak; the committed MuJoCo fits and transfers used the old timing.

    • Keep (recommended). The old timing is a bug (a force computed from last step's positions); the new numbers are the correct ones. Cost: the committed MuJoCo fits/transfers should be re-run when convenient (hours of MuJoCo time, not measured here); nothing else changes.
    • Revert. Hand-edit softsim/backends/mujoco/backend.py (it shares the file with prescribed motion). Keeps old numbers, keeps the bug.

    Decision: Keep the fix (my recommendation, applied 2026-11-03 under your "follow your recommendations"). Why: the old timing used last step's positions, a bug. Re-run on 2026-11-03 from a frozen copy of the package: the bending, inflation and PneuNet transfers reproduce the committed numbers (bending unchanged; inflation E 23.66 vs 23.7 kPa, errors 0.5/1.2/2.2% as before; PneuNet body E 91.0 kPa, layer 1.76 MPa, cost 2.9%, checks 3.2% and 4.3%, as before), so the timing fix moves the transfers by less than their rounding and the documented tables stand. The Ecoflex 00-10 drop transfer (transfer_ecoflex_00_10_mujoco.yaml, 40 evaluations, fresh output folder) reproduces the committed run to every digit (E 64.89 kPa, g 0.678, tau 7.82 ms, cost 0.0593, 8.6% / 3.3%), so the numbers in docs/experiments.md are those of the current code. ecoflex_00_10_drop.yaml (fit against the recordings, 50 evaluations) is not re-run.

    2. Joint drop + datasheet fit: stop or continue¶

    The 80-evaluation joint fit is nearly flat (E0 190 kPa, g 0.60, τ 0.58 ms, cost 0.108) but not proven converged; at 40 evaluations it clearly was not.

    • Continue to 160 evaluations (80 more evaluations at 60–70 s each on the GPU box, so 1.5–2 h): settles whether the datasheet term and the drop cases are compatible.
    • Stop (recommended). The cost is flat to the third digit; the remaining mismatch (the 20 g first contact 14%) looks structural (the card has one Prony term) rather than unconverged, so more evaluations buy little. A better use of the time is a second Prony term, which changes the card, so it is your call.

    Decision: Stop (recommendation applied 2026-11-03). Why: cost flat to the third digit; the residual looks structural (one Prony term). A second Prony term changes the card, so it waits for you.

    4. M8 rod backend: which library¶

    M8 wants a rod (Cosserat) backend for slender bodies, cross-checked against FEM.

    • SOFA BeamAdapter (installed with the v26.06 plugins; no new dependency): fits the existing SOFA backend; Euler–Bernoulli-like beams with fixed stiffness.
    • PyElastica: a real Cosserat rod (shear, twist, dynamics), pip-installable; a new dependency and a new backend to write.
    • Own implementation: most control, most work, and a verification burden (it would need its own analytic checks).

    Recommendation: BeamAdapter first, because it is already installed and a cantilever has exact solutions to check it against; PyElastica only if a quantity BeamAdapter cannot give (large shear, twist) is needed.

    Decision: SOFA BeamAdapter first (recommendation applied 2026-11-03). Why: already installed, no new dependency, cantilever has exact solutions to check against.

    5. Rigid-tendon option for cables¶

    A cable whose tendon stiffness tends to infinity (stroke imposed exactly) is the one case where an implicit SOFA CableConstraint (displacement type) would beat the explicit force, which is unstable for stiff tendons. It changes the cable model (today stiffness must be finite) and is SOFA-only.

    • Add it (config: stiffness: rigid): lets SOFA run stroke-controlled cables at any stiffness; the reference already has a stiff-tendon test to compare with. Cost: about a day, SOFA-only.
    • Skip (recommended for now). No scenario needs it; finite tendon stiffness is the physical case.

    Decision: Skip (recommendation applied 2026-11-03). Why: no scenario needs it; finite tendon stiffness is the physical case.

    6. The reference's static friction creeps, at a rate set by the time step¶

    The fem backend smooths Coulomb friction with IPC's mollifier below a slip of SLIP_TOLERANCE = 1 um per step (softsim/backends/fem/backend.py): below that, friction is mu N (2 y - y^2) with y = slip / 1 um, a spring rather than a stick. A contact held at a fraction r = F / (mu N) of its limit slips y = 1 - sqrt(1 - r) um every step, so it creeps at y x 1 um / dt. Measured on the sliding-block benchmark at r = 0.71 (benchmarks/sticking_block.yaml): 0.233 mm/s at dt 2 ms and 0.117 mm/s at 4 ms (uniform-r prediction 0.233 and 0.116). MuJoCo, known for soft-constraint creep, creeps less on this case (0.13 mm/s); SOFA least. Sliding is unaffected: all three backends are within 0.8-0.94% of exact Coulomb sliding (benchmarks/sliding_block.yaml). The finite-element grasps (docs/gripper.md) hold for seconds with the same smoothing, so part of their sink is this creep.

    Done meanwhile (no default changed): contact.slip_speed makes the tolerance a speed (slip_speed x dt, the IPC convention), so the creep is y x slip_speed at any step. Measured, 5 mm block, slip_speed 1e-5 m/s: 0.0052 mm/s at dt 2 ms and 0.0053 mm/s at 4 ms; sliding unchanged (70.45 mm). tests/test_friction.py checks both behaviours.

    • Make slip_speed 1e-5 m/s the default (recommended). Creep 45x lower at dt 2 ms and independent of the step. Cost: the finite-element grasps (about an hour each on the GPU machine) should be re-run, and their numbers in docs/gripper.md may move.
    • Keep the per-step default, and state the creep rate with every finite-element grasp.

    Decision: make slip_speed 1e-5 m/s the default (2026-11-11, agreed with you). contact.slip_speed: null restores the old per-step smoothing. Still to do: re-run the finite-element grasps on the GPU machine and update docs/gripper.md.
