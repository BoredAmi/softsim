    Drop test¶

    A rigid weight falls onto a soft specimen resting on a table (softsim/scenarios/drop_test.py, configs in scenarios/drop_test/).

    Reference dataset¶

    Czopek P., Superczynska P., Marcinkowska A., Gajewski P., Bernat J., Records of falling mass on Ecoflex 00-10 cylinder, Zenodo, doi:10.5281/zenodo.23185808, CC-BY-4.0.

    • Specimen: Ecoflex 00-10 cylinder, D 40 mm, H 17 mm, 19.49 g. That implies 912–917 kg/m³ against 1040 kg/m³ on the datasheet. The measured mass is used, and the mismatch is reported as a finding.
    • Drop: from 38 cm, so the impact speed is about 2.73 m/s (air drag neglected).
    • Weights:
    Weight Diameter Height Notes
    20 g 22.5 mm 5.6 mm brass
    50 g 27 mm 9.9 mm brass
    100 g 32 mm 18 mm black, no knob; hits the specimen edge
    200 g 40 mm 24 mm black; tilted edge impact
    500 g 43 mm 38 mm black, mushroom knob, wider than the specimen

    What we measured from the data (Step 0)¶

    • Frame rate is 2999.3 fps. The overlay frame/017866 … T=<s> gives 200 frames in 66.68 ms, and the zip frames are consecutive camera frames. The zips contain 800×800 RGB TIFFs (698 / 1106 / 786 / 1156 / 1506 frames for 20–500 g); the mp4 previews are the same frames, lossy, played at 12 fps.
    • Scale is about 5.3 px/mm (about 0.19 mm/px). The camera is slightly elevated, so the top face is visible and the weight's bottom edge is partly hidden. Track the weight's top edge or knob instead (it is rigid, so it moves the same distance).
    • 20 g preview, rough tracking: contact to peak takes about 7–9 frames (2.3–3 ms), peak indentation is about 4 mm, then the weight rebounds about 5 mm and settles.
    • 500 g contact sheet: the weight lands tilted, bounces, then topples off the cylinder. The specimen leaves the table about 60 ms after contact. (M3 wrongly attributed this to the table moving; the specimen does hop, pulling up the cloth it sticks to. See experiments.md.)
    • Full tracking results (contact, impact speed, offset, spin, support motion) are in experiments.md.

    Scenario¶

    The simulation starts with the weight's lowest point drop.gap (0.5 mm) above the specimen, moving at the free-fall speed for the remaining height. Parameters: weight preset or custom body, drop height or initial speed, xy offset, tilt about x and y, and initial angular velocity. The specimen can be base: free (on the table, with friction) or base: bonded (bottom face pinned).

    Support (optional). With support.enabled: true the specimen sits on a thin carrier plate (default 80 × 80 × 2 mm, 5 g) that can lift off the table against a spring and damper (support.stiffness, support.damping). It is a lumped model of the cloth in the recordings, which the tacky specimen drags up when it hops. It needs a backend with mount: slider_z (MuJoCo). Give the specimen material an adhesion so it sticks to the plate and the weight:

    support: {enabled: true, stiffness: 32.9 N/m, damping: 0.346 N*s/m, mass: 45.3 g}
specimen:
  material: ecoflex_00_10_drop_fit_tacky   # the drop fit plus fitted tack: strength 14.6 kPa, range 0.29 mm

    These are the values fitted to the recordings (see experiments.md); sweeps/fitted_tack_support.yaml runs all five weights with them.

    Metric Meaning
    indentation undeformed contact surface minus the weight's bottom-centre height
    compression downward displacement of the specimen's top-centre vertex
    bulge_diameter twice the maximum radial vertex distance
    specimen_min_z, specimen_max_z lift-off and top height
    contact_force_z specimen on weight (negative while adhesion pulls)
    ground_force_z ground (or support plate) on specimen
    support_z support plate height (with support.enabled)
    weight_z, weight_vz, weight_tilt weight state

    Summary: peak_indentation, t_to_peak, first_contact_duration, rebound_speed, restitution, peak_ground_force, max_bulge_diameter, specimen_lift_off, final_indentation, settling_time.

    Action drop(preset=, mass=, height=, offset=, tilt=) rebuilds the scene and drops again.

    M1 results: uncalibrated (superseded by M3 calibration)¶

    These use the ecoflex_00_10 preset (stable Neo-Hookean, E = 91 kPa from the datasheet, ν = 0.45, Rayleigh β = 2 ms placeholder), a 3.5 mm mesh, dt = 0.1 ms, contact timeconst 0.2 ms, 250 ms per run (softsim sweep scenarios/drop_test/sweeps/all_weights.yaml --jobs 5):

    Weight Peak indentation [mm] t to peak [ms] 1st contact [ms] Rebound [m/s] e Peak ground force [N] Max bulge Ø [mm] Lift-off [mm] Wall [s]
    20 g 1.97 2.0 5.0 0.49 0.18 36 42.0 1.3 68
    50 g 3.45 3.0 7.0 0.69 0.25 57 43.7 3.3 66
    100 g 4.72 3.6 8.0 0.83 0.30 87 46.0 7.0 63
    200 g 6.53 4.4 10.2 0.97 0.36 148 48.7 11.0 53
    500 g 10.29 6.4 14.6 1.17 0.43 291 53.6 16.1 47

    (Five runs in parallel on 8 cores; a single run takes about 40–50 s.)

    Compared with the 20 g preview, the model is too stiff: about 2 mm against about 4 mm of peak indentation. It is also too elastic: it rebounds at 0.49 m/s against roughly 0.3 m/s estimated from the rebound height. The restitution rising with mass is a signature of Rayleigh damping (heavier weights mean slower impacts, which are damped less). Whether real Ecoflex shows this is a key question for the M3 comparison. M3 calibration (experiments.md) fits E = 54.8 kPa and β = 0.31 ms (preset ecoflex_00_10_drop_fit, 5 mm mesh). That matches the 20 g and 50 g first impacts within 3–15%, but not the 500 g, which in reality is far stiffer and faster than Neo-Hookean + Rayleigh allows. The 100 g and 200 g recordings are off-centre edge impacts and can't be used. M4 repeated the fit on SOFA with an Ogden material: 20 and 50 g within 3–11%, 500 g still not reproduced (experiments.md).
