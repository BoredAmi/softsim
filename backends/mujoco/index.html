    MuJoCo backend: physics notes and limits¶

    Verified against MuJoCo 3.17.0, the pinned range (mujoco>=3.17,<3.18). The flex API changes every release, so read the changelog before bumping.

    What is used¶

    softsim MuJoCo
    Soft body flexcomp type="direct" dim="3" with our tetrahedral mesh; one 3-dof body per vertex
    stvk default flex elasticity: Saint Venant–Kirchhoff, linear tetrahedra
    stable_neo_hookean Stable Neo-Hookean (Smith et al. 2018), MjsFlex.elastic3d = 1 (experimental since 3.15, spec-only, not in MJCF). Classic neo_hookean (ln J energy) is not available and is refused.
    rayleigh.beta, kelvin_voigt flex damping (stiffness-proportional; Kelvin–Voigt maps to beta = eta / E)
    prony (on stable_neo_hookean) the flex carries the long-term law (shear modulus × (1 − Σg), bulk unchanged); the Maxwell-branch overstress of every tetrahedron (softsim/materials/viscoelastic.py, needs the fem extra) is applied as explicit vertex forces (xfrc_applied) before every step
    base: bonded <pin> on the bottom-face vertices
    Contact soft constraints solref = (timeconst, dampratio), solimp; friction combines by max
    adhesion (material, ground) external forces (xfrc_applied) on surface vertices and rigid bodies, recomputed before every step from analytic signed distances (softsim/contact/adhesion.py)
    mount: slider_z slide joint along z with stiffness, damping, springref = 0 (rest = initial pose)
    Integrator discrete (new in 3.13, Sept 2026, experimental): implicit in position, so flex stiffness has no explicit stability limit

    Not supported, and refused: small-strain linear elasticity, Mooney-Rivlin, Yeoh, Ogden, Gent, Arruda–Boyce, mass-proportional damping, magneto-active coupling, negative Poisson's ratio, any integrator other than discrete, and the PGS solver.

    Measured behaviour (drop test, this repo)¶

    • Material mapping is correct. Under a frictionless 3% uniaxial compression, the result matches δ = m·g·H/(E·A) within 0.7–1.8% for both stable_neo_hookean and stvk (tests/test_drop_test.py::test_static_uniaxial_compression).
    • The timestep is not the accuracy bottleneck. With the contact model held fixed, peak indentation at dt = 0.1 ms and 0.05 ms differs by 0.2–0.7%. Use dt ≤ 0.1 ms for impacts; 0.2 ms is within about 3%.
    • Contact compliance matters as much as the material. Peak indentation for 20 g changes by about 10% between timeconst 0.2 ms and 0.4 ms. Penetration scales with roughly v·timeconst. Keep timeconst fixed in configs (scenario default 0.2 ms) and treat it as a model parameter; auto (= 2·dt) couples it to the timestep.
    • Results are not mesh-converged. Linear tetrahedra at ν = 0.45 lock: peak indentation grows 10–15% per refinement step (5 → 3.5 → 2.5 mm). A calibrated modulus is therefore an effective value for one mesh resolution; fitted presets must record their mesh in provenance. Pushing ν toward Ecoflex's real ~0.49 makes this worse.
    • StVK collapses in compression. With 200 g and 500 g, StVK lets the weight crush through the full 17 mm. Stable Neo-Hookean stays physical (8 and 13 mm in the Step 0 probe) and is the default. Naming: this was called neo_hookean until M4; it is the stable variant, a different energy at large strain from classic Neo-Hookean, so it now has its own model name.
    • The 500 g misfit is not a MuJoCo limitation. Expect the 200–500 g cases to fit worse than 20–50 g. M4 tested this on SOFA: an Ogden fit doesn't reproduce 500 g either, and actually prefers less large-strain stiffening than Neo-Hookean. Compressibility (ν = 0.45) and missing rate dependence matter more; see experiments.md.
    • Native damping is a single Rayleigh beta, a crude proxy for viscoelasticity: the loss factor grows linearly with frequency. Beta and E trade off strongly over a millisecond impact. Prony cards avoid this.
    • Prony viscoelasticity is explicit. Its overstress lags one step, so its stiffness Σg·μ0 must be resolved by the time step (softsim check reports ω·dt). This is no issue at drop-test steps: the limit is about 0.25 ms on a 3 mm mesh. The forces match the homogeneous reference exactly under uniform deformation (tests/test_viscoelastic.py). A 50 g drop with g = 0.6 at τ = 2 ms peaks between the instantaneous and the long-term material, at 5.38 mm on MuJoCo and 5.39 mm on SOFA.
    • Friction changes the impact a lot. 50 g drop, no material damping, 5 mm mesh: peak 4.89, 5.72 and 6.80 mm at sliding friction 1, 0.3 and 0. The bulging specimen slides against the weight and the table, and that matters as much as the modulus.
    • An undamped impact loses energy that a converged solver keeps. See Energy and contact fidelity below. Fitted damping (β) is therefore an effective value on top of MuJoCo's own losses.
    • Mesh stiffness against a converged reference: for a static flat punch (small strain) the 5 mm mesh is 20–60% stiffer than the locking-free reference (reference_fem.md).
    • Peak contact force is not converged. It is a first-contact spike on tiny vertex masses and changes with dt (70–140 N for 20 g). Use indentation, timing, rebound and ground force for calibration.
    • Specimen lift-off. In the free-base configuration the specimen's elastic recoil can launch it off the rigid table after the weight separates (1 mm at 20 g up to 16 mm at 500 g with the uncalibrated preset). It is reported as specimen_lift_off. The real specimens do hop, by 8–20 mm, but for a different reason: the tacky specimen is pulled up by the weight stuck to it, and drags the cloth under it along. Reproducing that needs adhesion and the compliant support (below). Correction history: M3 read the specimen's hop as table motion; that was wrong. See experiments.md.
    • Solver warnings. At large dt on finer meshes, the inner M+K solve can run out of CG iterations (mjWARN_INERTIA). softsim reports this as a backend-warnings finding; raise simulation.iterations or lower dt.
    • Divergence. MuJoCo resets the state itself on NaN; softsim detects this through the bad-qacc/qpos/qvel warning counters and raises StabilityError.

    Energy and contact fidelity¶

    How much of an impact's energy MuJoCo loses that it shouldn't, measured with purely elastic cards (no damping of any kind) against the locking-free 3D reference (tools/contact_restitution.py, 2026-10-31).

    1. Flex elasticity is numerically damped like backward Euler. A free axial vibration of the bonded specimen (no contact, no damping, 6.25 ms period) loses energy per cycle as predicted by a damping ratio ζ ≈ ω·dt/2:

    Timestep 100 µs 50 µs 25 µs 12.5 µs
    Energy lost per cycle 52% 30% 15% 6.5%
    1 − exp(−4πζ), ζ = ω·dt/2 47% 27% 15% 7.6%

    For the mode you care about (angular frequency ω), pick dt so that ω·dt/2 is well below the material damping you want to model.

    2. Impacts: friction slip costs most. A centred 50 g weight hits the drop specimen at 2.7 m/s. Restitution e = rebound speed / impact speed:

    Contact Reference (fem) MuJoCo, 5 mm mesh MuJoCo, 3.5 / 2.5 mm
    Frictionless 0.869 0.791 0.839 / 0.838
    Sticking, μ = 1 0.905 0.71–0.75 –
    • Contact settings don't matter. e stays at 0.71–0.75 over damping ratio 1 to 0.05, time constant 0.1–0.2 ms and timestep 50 to 6 µs.
    • Without friction MuJoCo is within 3.5% at meshes of 3.5 mm or finer. At 5 mm, each surface vertex's impact on the weight is plastic, and that costs about 6% more.
    • With friction it loses about twice as much. The reference sticks, which does no work. MuJoCo's soft friction constraint slips while the specimen bulges under the weight, and the slip dissipates.
    • The best available mitigation: contact.cone: elliptic with contact.impratio: 10 (10–100 are equivalent). This raises e from 0.748 to 0.78 at 5 mm. MuJoCo's no-slip solver would remove the slip, but it is not supported with flex under the discrete integrator. The defaults stay pyramidal and 1, which is what every fit in this repository used.

    3. Locking: at ν = 0.45 the 5 mm mesh's peak compression is 24% below the reference's (4.9 against 6.5 mm with sticking).

    These losses are why MuJoCo's fitted cards are effective. Transfer a physical card (materials.md) rather than using its values directly.

    Adhesion (tack)¶

    • Model: an exponential cohesive law in the normal direction, t(g) = σ (g/δ) e^(1 − g/δ), acting on the surface area that faces the counterpart. A bond forms on contact and breaks for good beyond 6δ. Work of separation is e·σ·δ per area. See softsim.materials.Adhesion.
    • Explicit. The forces are computed from the state at the start of each step, so the adhesive stiffness e·σ/δ per vertex area must be resolvable: softsim check reports adhesion-stiff (ω·dt > 0.3) and refuses adhesion-unstable (ω·dt > 1). Lower the timestep or raise range.
    • No damping of its own. A body held only by adhesion oscillates on the bond undamped (Rayleigh β damps strain, not rigid motion). The energy still stored in a bond when it breaks is the only loss. Rate-dependent tack is not modelled.
    • Verified (tests/test_adhesion.py): a stiff block held to the ground, and a disc held to a block, under slowly reversed gravity settle at the analytic opening of the traction law within 2%. Above the bond capacity the block tears off. The reported contact force equals the load.
    • Mesh dependence. Tractions are lumped on surface vertices, so the bonded area is resolved at the mesh size (a 12 mm disc on a 5 mm mesh bonds about 10% too much area). More importantly, peeling is only mesh-converged when the cohesive zone, l ≈ E·(e·σ·δ)/σ², spans several elements. For the drop-test fit (σ ≈ 14 kPa, δ = 0.3 mm, E ≈ 73 kPa) that is about 4 mm, less than the 5 mm mesh. On 5 mm the bond acts all-or-nothing; on 3.5 mm it peels and lets the 20 g weight go. Fitted tack parameters are therefore effective values for the mesh they were fitted on, like the fitted moduli.

    Performance¶

    762-vertex cylinder (3.5 mm mesh), dt = 0.1 ms, one CPU core: about 0.005× real time, so a 250 ms drop takes about 50 s. The 5 mm mesh is about 3× faster. Sweeps run in parallel processes (--jobs). No GPU is used or needed.
