    SOFA backend: setup, physics notes and limits¶

    Verified against the SOFA v26.06.00 Linux binary release built for Python 3.10, with its bundled SofaPython3. It runs on CPU; no GPU is needed.

    Install¶

    # download and unzip the release, e.g. into ~/.local/opt/sofa
curl -LO https://github.com/sofa-framework/sofa/releases/download/v26.06.00/SOFA_v26.06.00_Linux_Python3.10.zip
mkdir -p ~/.local/opt/sofa && unzip SOFA_v26.06.00_Linux_Python3.10.zip -d ~/.local/opt/sofa
export SOFA_ROOT=~/.local/opt/sofa/SOFA_v26.06.00_Linux        # add to your shell profile
softsim run scenarios/drop_test/cases/w020.yaml --backend sofa --set simulation.timestep=5e-5

    softsim finds SofaPython3 through $SOFA_ROOT. Without it, the SOFA tests skip and softsim check still reports what SOFA would support.

    Scene¶

    softsim SOFA
    Soft body TetrahedronSetTopologyContainer, MechanicalObject, lumped MeshMatrixMass, EulerImplicitSolver, SparseLDLSolver
    linear TetrahedronFEMForceField method="small"
    stvk, neo_hookean, stable_neo_hookean TetrahedronHyperelasticityFEMForceField, ParameterSet [μ, λ]
    mooney_rivlin same force field, [C10, C01, bulk]
    ogden (one term) same force field, [μ·α, α, bulk] (SOFA writes the term as μ₁/α²)
    Tension-controlled cable springs between the via points (StiffSpringForceField on a BarycentricMapping child) whose rest lengths are reset every step to length - T / k: the force is the tension at the current positions and the cable's tangent stiffness T (I - u u^T) / l is in the implicit matrix and the Rayleigh damping (k is 1% of T per via-point spacing, so the axial stiffness is negligible). An explicit follower force (a ConstantForceField, as before; identical numbers to the force-type CableConstraint) swung the two-cable arm in a limit cycle, 0.6-3.9 mm peak to peak at every step from 10 ms to 0.25 ms, because the elastic law's geometric stiffness is implicit and the cable stiffness that balances it was not (control.md). Stroke-controlled and anchored cables, pressure and region forces are still ConstantForceFields
    Rayleigh α, β; Kelvin-Voigt EulerImplicitSolver rayleighMass/rayleighStiffness (Kelvin-Voigt: β = η/E). α is checked against a free-falling body with linear drag (v' = g − αv): the velocity matches the implicit-Euler recurrence v ← (v + g dt)/(1 + α dt) to 1e-12 and the continuous solution to 1% at every step (first order in dt), 0.1% at the end (tests/test_sofa_backend.py). One damping per body: the solver owns it, and a force field's own rayleighStiffness has no effect on the elastic force fields (measured), so zones with their own damping are refused
    prony the force field carries the long-term law; the Maxwell-branch overstress (softsim/materials/viscoelastic.py, the same code as on MuJoCo) goes through a ConstantForceField updated before every step. SOFA's own MJEDTetrahedralForceField (SofaMJEDFEM) is not used: it relaxes the whole stress including the volumetric part, and its laws use non-isochoric invariants (its Mooney-Rivlin has a stress at rest)
    base: bonded FixedProjectiveConstraint
    Rigid body Rigid3d DOF at the centre of mass, UniformMass with inertia, triangulated parts (RigidMapping)
    Contact FreeMotionAnimationLoop, BlockGaussSeidelConstraintSolver, MinProximityIntersection, frictional Lagrange contact

    Refused: Yeoh, Gent, Arruda–Boyce, multi-term Ogden, torsional and rolling friction, self-collision, magneto-active coupling. A card with one of these laws can be projected onto one SOFA has (softsim material project).

    Verified¶

    • Material mappings. All of linear, neo_hookean, stable_neo_hookean, mooney_rivlin and ogden are checked against analytic 1% uniaxial compression with forces applied directly to nodes (no contact), within 4% (tests/test_sofa_backend.py). Mooney-Rivlin's energy function in the SOFA sources has an integer division (pow(J, -2/3) evaluates to J⁰), but its forces were checked: zero stress at rest and the standard stiffness μ = 2(C10 + C01).
    • Timestep. For the 20 g drop, dt = 0.1 ms and 0.05 ms agree within 2%. The 500 g full-face impact diverges at 0.1 ms (a rim vertex blows up at about 6 ms), and is stable at 0.05 ms. It can still diverge at 0.05 ms with stiff, heavily damped parameter sets (E = 100 kPa, β = 4 ms).
    • Blow-up guard. A diverging FEM state floods the constraint solver with contacts, and SOFA segfaults while allocating the compliance matrix before any NaN shows up. The backend therefore stops stepping once any vertex or rigid body moves faster than SOFTSIM_SOFA_MAX_SPEED (default 100 m/s) and reports the run as diverged (StabilityError), like a NaN.

    Known limits¶

    • Speed. About 0.001× real time for the drop test on a 5 mm mesh: a 22 ms impact takes 30 s, or 90 s at dt = 0.05 ms. MuJoCo is about 10× faster.
    • Line-line collision is off for soft bodies. It's about 3× faster, and the drop test result didn't change.
    • Frictionless plate test (explained: friction). The old "7% stiff, too much spreading" comes from a plate that was not frictionless: SOFA applies the scene's largest coefficient to every pair, so a 0.8 on any body, the ground or the plate sticks the plate to the specimen's top face and the ground. A weighted plate on a cylinder (R 20 mm, H 17 mm, stable Neo-Hookean 50 kPa, ν 0.45, 2% load, rim measured as the mean radius), contact against the same load as nodal forces on the same mesh (tests/test_sofa_backend.py, 8 mm mesh):
    Friction on the ground and plate Compression, contact / no contact Rim spreading, contact / no contact
    0 1.108 / 1.101 (+0.6%) 1.012% / 1.011% (rim radius change)
    0.01 0.996 of the no-contact one 0.988
    0.05 0.976 0.959
    0.2 0.898 0.838
    0.8 0.670 (-33%) 0.48% (half)

    (Rows 0.01 to 0.2 are contact over no contact; the 0.8 row is at 8 mm for both, with 6 mm giving 0.765 and ground only, plate only and both identical.) So the 7% corresponds to a coefficient of about 0.1, and friction on either of the pair acts. At 0 the defaults already agree to 0.6% (0.4% at 6 mm), so the other suspects need no fix: the contact distance (0.1 mm per surface, 1.2% of 17 mm at most, and below the 0.6% seen), solver tolerance and iterations (defaults, converged), mesh coarseness (6 and 8 mm agree), and the analytic reference's boundary (free-slip faces on both sides; the no-contact FEM sits 1% above the analytic rim spreading of 0.91% at either mesh, the discretisation and not the contact). Keep one friction coefficient per scene on SOFA. - Frictionless impacts: compare with every pair frictionless. With purely elastic cards, the centred 50 g drop at 2.7 m/s against the locking-free 3D reference (tools/contact_restitution.py; restitution e = rebound speed / impact speed):

    Contact Reference SOFA, 5 mm MuJoCo, 5 mm
    Sticking, μ = 1 0.905 0.794 0.73–0.75
    Every pair frictionless 0.823, peak 7.53 mm 0.795 (−3.4%), peak 6.45 mm (−14%) –
    Specimen and ground frictionless, the weight at 0.5 0.869 0.650, peak 5.1 mm (not a like-for-like case) 0.791

    The last row was long read as "SOFA loses 58% of the energy frictionless". It doesn't: SOFA applies the scene's largest friction coefficient to every pair, so the weight's 0.5 also acted between specimen and ground, where the reference and MuJoCo (friction per pair) had none. With every pair frictionless SOFA loses 3.4% more than the reference, and its peak is 14% shallow, the linear tetrahedra's stiffness at 5 mm. Halving the step (0.784) or tightening the constraint tolerance to 1e-9 does not change that. Keep one friction coefficient per scene on SOFA. With sticking friction SOFA loses less than MuJoCo. That is why its transfer of the physical card needs less extra relaxation (g 0.77 against 0.94, experiments.md). - Rim flare through the ground (fixed: the alarm distance follows the per-step travel). In the 500 g drop with the fitted Ogden material the specimen's top rim rolls under the specimen, and then snaps back onto the ground at about 12 m/s: 0.6 mm per 50 µs step, while the intersection's alarm distance was 0.4 mm. A point farther than the alarm distance from the ground at the start of a step gets no contact, ends it below the surface, and MinProximityIntersection's normal then points away from the far side and keeps it there: one vertex (a surface vertex that was on the top face at rest) went 2.8 mm through at 8.3 ms (8.5 mm by 22 ms), with no inverted element. Measured on the 5 mm mesh, 8.8 ms (the artefact does not appear at 5.5 mm and coarser, and does not in a head-on drop of a block at 6 m/s per 0.5 ms steps):

    Run Deepest vertex through the ground Peak compression
    Before (alarm 0.4 mm) 2.83 mm 13.13 mm
    Alarm 1.5 mm 0.005 mm 13.16 mm
    Time step 25 µs (0.3 mm per step) 0.002 mm 13.17 mm
    Constraint solver 1000 iterations, tolerance 1e-10 8.13 mm (worse) 13.19 mm
    Every pair frictionless 0.002 mm (no rim snap: the trajectory differs, peak 16.08 mm) 16.08 mm
    Alarm = max(0.4 mm, contact distance + fastest vertex speed × dt) 0.006 mm 13.14 mm

    The solver's limits are not the cause (above), nor is the ground or the collision models: the ground is a 30 m triangle the rim cannot leave (the specimen reaches 42 mm), and every surface vertex carries a point and a triangle model. The backend now sets the alarm distance at each step from the fastest vertex speed (SofaBackend._alarm; SOFTSIM_SOFA_ADAPTIVE_ALARM=0 restores the fixed distance, SOFTSIM_SOFA_ALARM_FACTOR scales the travel, default 1). It costs 12% more CPU time in this run (a factor 2, which fixes it as well, 60%). Below 6 m/s at 50 µs the nominal 0.4 mm applies and nothing changes. ground_penetration in calibration histories still records any left. - One friction coefficient (the largest in the scene) applies to all pairs; softsim check and every run warn (single-friction) when the scene's coefficients differ. MuJoCo's contact compliance settings (contact.timeconst, dampratio, solimp) don't apply here: SOFA contacts are hard constraints. simulation.integrator and solver are MuJoCo settings and are ignored. - Contact forces come from momentum balance per body: net force only, at the centre of mass, with no centre of pressure or torque. - Ground. The ground is one large triangle. A shared triangle edge under the specimen let the vertex sitting exactly on it slip through (0.5 mm penetration) before this was fixed. - Fitted parameters are backend-specific. With the same E, β and mesh, SOFA and MuJoCo differ by about 30% in peak indentation for the 20 g drop, and the stable-NH drop-test fits end at E = 105 kPa (SOFA) vs 55 kPa (MuJoCo). The SOFA fits and their comparison are in experiments.md; preset ecoflex_00_10_drop_fit_sofa_ogden is the SOFA one-term Ogden fit. The differences are hard vs compliant contact and integrator damping (MuJoCo's contact alone adds about 0.5 mm of penetration). Calibrate per backend.

    Rods (BeamAdapter)¶

    softsim.backends.sofa.rod is the bare clamped cantilever on BeamAdapter that the rod backend grew from (F L^3 / (3 E I) to 0.2%, tests/test_rod_sofa.py). The scene backend is --backend rod: see rod.md.
