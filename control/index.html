    Inverse control (M11, first piece)¶

    Given a scenario with actuators and a target for one or more of its outputs, find the actuator values that reach it at equilibrium. Code: softsim/control/inverse.py; tests: tests/test_inverse.py.

    softsim inverse jobs/inverse_cable_cantilever.yaml     # tension for a 2 degree tip tilt
softsim inverse jobs/inverse_cable_arm.yaml            # two cables, tip x and z

    from softsim.control.inverse import solve_inverse

res = solve_inverse("scenarios/actuation/cable_cantilever.yaml", {"bend_angle": "2 deg"},
                    ["cable"], {"cable": (0, "0.7 N")}, overrides=["body.mesh.size=4mm"])
res.values, res.status, res.residual, res.simulations

    A job file names the config, overrides, backend, targets (any output column of the scenario, such as tip_x, tip_z, bend_angle, chord_angle; values with units), actuators (name: [lower, upper], finite) and optionally tolerance, max_evaluations, initial, scales and the settling options below. The result is printed as JSON: values, the outputs reached, errors, residual, status, at_bounds, simulations, steps, wall_time.

    How it solves¶

    1. The equilibrium map. The actuators are held at constant values (Simulation.apply_controls), the scene runs until every target output has stopped changing, and the outputs are read. One Simulation is built and reused: each evaluation continues from the previous equilibrium (warm_start; reset() is called only at the start, or for every evaluation with warm_start: false). For an elastic body the equilibrium does not depend on the path, so this only saves time. Actuators that are not unknowns are held at their value at t = 0.
    2. Settling. An elastic body rings forever, so the solve gives the body (and each zone without damping of its own) stiffness-proportional damping: settle_damping, Rayleigh beta, default 10 ms. A damping force vanishes at rest, so the equilibrium is where it was. MuJoCo has no mass-proportional damping, so the damping time scale has to suit the slowest mode: the cantilever (first mode ~20 Hz at 4 mm) settles with 10 ms; the two-segment arm (first mode ~1 Hz) needs 150 ms. The state counts as settled when every target output moves less than settle_tol (default 0.001) of its scale over the last settle_window (default 0.1 s). The window has to be longer than the slowest relaxation: with a 20 ms window the arm was declared settled while it had only just started to move and the finite-difference Jacobian was wrong (found while building this, see the arm job). Hitting max_settle is counted in unsettled.
    3. The solve. Scaled residuals (output - target) / scale (scale: 1 mm for lengths, 0.01 rad for angles, 1 mN for forces, 100 Pa for pressures; override with scales) are minimised by scipy.optimize.least_squares (trust region, bounds) over the actuator values scaled to [0, 1] of their bounds, with a forward-difference Jacobian (step 2% of the range): one extra equilibrium per actuator per iteration. It stops when the largest scaled residual is below tolerance (default 0.05) or after max_evaluations equilibria.
    4. The outcome. status is converged; bounded_best (an actuator sits at a bound and the target is not reached: the bounded best is returned, at_bounds names it, no exception); max_evaluations; or stalled.

    Targets that do not determine the actuators are refused (fewer targets than actuators).

    Linearity. On the cantilever MuJoCo's own forward map gives 0.01296, 0.02583, 0.05130 rad at 0.1, 0.2, 0.4 N: the slope varies by 1.1% over the range, as Euler-Bernoulli says for small angles (theta = T d L / (E I)), and the inverse of twice the 0.1 N angle is 0.2 N within 4% (tested). MuJoCo's angle is 0.33 times the EB value at 4 mm (the locking documented in actuation.md).

    Conditioning. Two cables with the tip position as targets is an ill-posed problem unless the targets carry both degrees of freedom. On the arm, tip x and the tilt bend_angle fail: bend_angle is an unsigned angle, folded at zero, so the map has a kink. Tip x and tip z work, but z moves ~1.5 mm over the whole range of the cables (it carries the "S against C" shape in second order), so it is judged to 0.2 mm (scales); at a loose tolerance (0.3) the solution reached 0.435 / 0.144 N for the true 0.45 / 0.15 N, at 0.1 it reached 0.448 / 0.149 N. The tolerance bounds the residual, not the actuator error.

    Limits¶

    • Quasi-static only. Each answer is a held value that reaches the target after settling; the path (and the time it takes, which depends on the damping used to settle) is not planned. Transient control is the next M11 piece.
    • The values are the backend's. The MuJoCo flex locks (about 30% too stiff in bending at 2 mm, a factor 3 at the 4 mm used here for speed), so tensions found on it are not physical ones. For physical values use a card transferred to the backend (softsim transfer, actuation.md) and a mesh that has been checked against the reference, or solve on the reference backend, which is much slower.
    • One equilibrium per value. A structure that snaps or buckles can have several equilibria; the one found depends on the start, and the finite-difference Jacobian is wrong across a snap.
    • History-dependent bodies (contact friction, viscoelastic relaxation beyond max_settle) have no single equilibrium map; warm_start: false removes the dependence on the previous value only.
    • Cost. Each iteration costs 1 + n_actuators equilibria, each a settling run of 0.1-1 s of simulated time. Slow bodies (the arm) cost minutes. The SOFA fast path below removes the repeated solves; reduced models are not implemented.
    • Not done: constraints on outputs, bounds on rates, other backends than MuJoCo and SOFA in tests (backend= is passed through, untested here for the reference).

    SOFA fast path: SoftRobots.Inverse (--method sofa-qp)¶

    softsim inverse jobs/inverse_cable_cantilever_sofa.yaml                  # `method: sofa-qp` in the job
softsim inverse jobs/inverse_cable_cantilever.yaml --method sofa-qp      # or on the command line
softsim inverse jobs/inverse_cable_arm_sofa.yaml                         # two cables, tip x and z, one QP run
softsim inverse jobs/inverse_bending_sofa.yaml                           # a pressure (SurfacePressureActuator)
softsim inverse jobs/inverse_bending_angle_sofa.yaml                     # a tip tilt with a pressure
softsim inverse jobs/inverse_bending_cable_sofa.yaml                     # a pressure and a cable together
softsim inverse jobs/inverse_cable_stroke_sofa.yaml                      # a stroke-controlled cable

    from softsim.control.sofa_inverse import solve_inverse_sofa

res = solve_inverse_sofa("scenarios/actuation/cable_cantilever.yaml", {"tip_x": "2 mm"}, ["cable"],
                         {"cable": (0, "0.7 N")}, overrides=["body.mesh.size=4mm"])

    Code: softsim/control/sofa_inverse.py; tests: tests/test_sofa_inverse.py (skipped without SOFA). The result is an InverseResult like solve_inverse's (simulations is the number of settled QP runs).

    What it is. SOFA's own inverse solver does the inversion inside the constraint solve. The scene is the scenario's body (same mesh, material, fixed vertices) with QPInverseProblemSolver replacing the Gauss-Seidel solver, each cable a CableActuator (the via points a BarycentricMapping child of the body, pullPoint 1 mm behind the first via point) with minForce / maxForce from the job's bounds, and the target a PositionEffector on a barycentric point at the tip face's rest centroid. At each step the QP finds the cable forces that bring the effector to its goal at the end of the step, within the bounds; the run is stepped until forces and outputs have stopped moving (settle_window, settle_tol as above), and the tensions are read.

    • Units. CableActuator's force is the constraint impulse: the bounds are given to it as force * dt and the reported tension is lambda / dt (without the factor the bound is 1/dt times too loose: the 0.7 N limit becomes 7000 N and the tip flies off; a mutation of this makes the tests fail).
    • Targets. tip_x, tip_y, tip_z are the effector's goal (axes chosen with useDirections); there must be at least as many axes as cables. The effector is a barycentric point and the scenario's output is the face's area-weighted centroid, so the goal is corrected by the remaining error after each settled run (up to max_corrections; the cantilever needed none, the error of the point against the centroid being 2 um). bend_angle / chord_angle are not a position: the effector goal is moved along an axis no position target uses (angle_direction, +x by default; -x is tried when +x leaves every actuator at its lower bound, as for a tube that curls to -x) and the goals of all the targets are found by a Broyden iteration on the angle / position read from the vertices (a secant for one angle: 2 QP runs for the 2 degree job). This works with any actuator (a pressure, a cable, both) and with angle and position targets together; each angle needs a free axis (at most three targets in all). It needs no second effector point (a pair of points on the tip face fixes the translation as well as the tilt, and a Rigid3d effector does not exist in this build: PositionEffector, CableEffector, VolumeEffector and BarycentricCenterEffector are the registered ones), so the angle is the scenario's own metric, exactly. Other outputs are refused.
    • Step. SOFA is implicit, so the step is 2 ms (timestep), not the scenario's 0.1 ms; the body gets the same stiffness-proportional damping as the repeated solve (settle_damping, default 10 ms) so that it settles.
    • Pressure actuators. A pneumatic actuator (SurfacePressureActuator) is built on the actuator's cavity region (a child node with the cavity triangles, mapped to the body); bounds are pressures (cavity: [0 kPa, 10 kPa]) and the targets are positions (tip_x, tip_y, tip_z; mind the direction a robot bends: the bending tube curls to -x, the PneuNet to -z). Measured: the solver's lambda is an impulse, pressure * dt, like the cables (the bounds go in as pressure * dt, the pressure is lambda / dt: a run clamped at 2 kPa gives the tip the ordinary backend's explicit follower pressure gives at 2 kPa, -0.1230656 mm, to 7 digits). Two traps, both found by measurement: the cavity triangles need the opposite winding to softsim's (else the volume shrinks and the pressure clamps at 0), and in SI units the QP's matrices are tiny (the pressure's entry is ~4e-12), so qpOASES returns lambda = 0 for every reachable goal and bangs between 0 and the bound for an unreachable one. The effector weight scales the objective: 1e6 (qp_weight) is the default with pressures, 1e5 and 1e7 give the same pressure. Angle targets work with a pressure (below).
    • Mixed actuators. Pressures and cables can be solved together (one QPInverseProblemSolver, each actuator with its own impulse bounds), with qp_weight 1e6 as soon as one is a pressure (it does not hurt the cable's scaling). scenarios/inflation/bending_cable.yaml is the bending tube with a cable in its thin +x wall: the pressure curls the tip to -x and lengthens the tube, the cable pulls it to +x and shortens it, so tip x and z together fix both.
    • Stroke-controlled cables. A cable with control: stroke (tension k (L - L0 + s)) is solved as a tension and the stroke read back as s = T / k - (L - L0) from the final vertices; the bounds are strokes (cable: [0 mm, 3 mm]) and the QP's tension bounds k (s_bound + L - L0) are re-set after each run at the length reached (the length depends on the tension, so the bound is found by a secant: 3 runs when a bound is active; a bound that is not active needs no iteration).
    • Outcome. Same statuses as above. A goal beyond the bounds leaves the cable at its bound (bounded_best, at_bounds): no second solve is made once a force is saturated.

    The tensions agree to 0.6% (angle target; the two stop at residuals of 0.014 and 0.009 of a 0.01 rad scale, i.e. 0.4% in angle) and 1.2% (position target; the repeated solve stopped 23 um short of the target, and scaling its tension by the ratio of the tips gives 0.5517 N against the QP's 0.5575 N: 1%). The QP's tension, applied as the ordinary backend's explicit cable force, puts the tip within 0.05 mm of the 2 mm target (tested). The arm's targets are the tip MuJoCo reaches at 0.45 / 0.15 N on the same mesh, so the 0.07% agreement is across backends.

    The pressure targets are the tips the same model reaches at a held pressure: the bending tube's from the ordinary SOFA backend (explicit follower pressure, 2 ms steps, 59 s), the PneuNet's from a QP run clamped at 5 kPa (the explicit backend is too slow at 1.5 mm; the clamped run was checked against it on the tube). The tube's tip moves only 0.3 mm at 4 kPa (stiff walls), so its scale is 0.01 mm, not the default 1 mm, which a run that never pressurised would satisfy.

    Why the arm stalled on the repeated solve (found and fixed). The ordinary SOFA backend used to apply the cable as an explicit follower force, and on the 6 mm arm that did not settle: the tip's x swung by 0.6-1.9 mm peak to peak indefinitely (period 0.39 s, 2.6 Hz, along the mesh diagonal), at every step from 10 ms to 0.25 ms, while MuJoCo settled. Measured on the 6 mm mesh at 0.45 / 0.15 N, Rayleigh beta 0.15 s:

    Run Tip x peak to peak, last seconds
    explicit cable, dt 10 / 5 / 1 / 0.25 ms 0.59 / 0.85 / 1.9 / 3.9 mm (grows as dt shrinks; still growing at 0.25 ms)
    explicit, no damping, 5 ms 1.8 mm
    explicit, beta 0.5 / 1.5 s, 5 ms regrows to 0.67 mm / decays (0.012 mm at 3.5 s)
    cable A alone, B alone, both at half tension (10 ms) settle
    both cables at twice the tension (10 ms) 1.7 mm
    released at 2 s (no cable) decays in 1 s
    force-type CableConstraint (SoftRobots) instead of the force field identical to the explicit run to 4 digits
    linear (small) or corotational (large, svd) elastic law, same load settles (tip x 1.80, 4.39, 4.74 mm)
    cable as springs in the implicit system (below), 5 / 2 / 1 ms settles; tip x 4.0025 mm, z 118.514 mm at every step

    So it is not the step size (it gets worse as the step shrinks, and extrapolating the positions to the end of the step for the force changes little), not the routing points (the cable's Jacobian is symmetric and positive semi-definite, checked against finite differences), and not the constraint form of the cable (identical). It needs both cables (the S shape) and the hyperelastic force field. The reading that fits all of it, and that the fix below supports (an inference, the hyperelastic tangent was not computed directly), is: the cable is a stabilising lateral stiffness T (I - u u^T) / l per segment, and under the arm's compression (0.6 N against a column whose Euler load is about 0.03 N) it balances the elastic force field's negative geometric stiffness. TetrahedronHyperelasticityFEMForceField has, we infer, that geometric part in its tangent, so the implicit matrix (and with it the Rayleigh beta K damping, and implicit Euler's own numerical damping) is nearly singular or indefinite in the bending mode, with the cable's compensating stiffness missing from it. The slowest modes are then undamped or anti-damped whatever the step: a limit cycle. The corotational field has no geometric stiffness in its tangent and the small-strain one no geometric effect at all, so they settle (but are wrong: 4.39 and 1.80 mm against 3.994 mm).

    The backend now puts a tension-controlled cable into the implicit system: springs between the via points (mapped on the mesh) whose rest lengths are reset every step to length - T / k, which gives the force T at the current positions and the tangent T (I - u u^T) / l as stiffness, with a negligible axial k (1% of T per via-point spacing). The arm now settles within 1 s at 5 ms to 1 ms steps (tip x 4.0025 mm, z 118.514 mm; the QP path's tip is 3.994 mm and 118.515 mm, MuJoCo's 3.994 mm: 8.5 um apart in x, and the tension is the same), and the repeated solve on SOFA now converges (table above). Stroke-controlled and anchored cables are still explicit follower forces.

    Limits.

    • Same model as the repeated solve, same locking. SOFA's linear tetrahedra lock in bending like MuJoCo's flex: the 4 mm cantilever is too stiff, and the tensions are those of the mesh (see actuation.md). The QP solves the model faster; it does not make the model more accurate.
    • Quasi-static. The forces are the held values at equilibrium; the path and the time to get there depend on the step and the damping. The QP is a per-step solution, not a trajectory plan.
    • The effector. A position (up to three axes) of one point. Angles are reached through that point's goal (the iteration above, a few extra QP runs), not as an orientation constraint of the QP; at most three targets (positions and angles) in all, an angle's direction must be a free axis, and the angle direction is a single sign for all angles. A tilt of an unreachable direction (e.g. +x for a tube that only curls to -x) is detected only when the actuators sit at their lower bound. Output constraints are not built. Fewer targets than actuators is refused (the repeated solve refuses it too).
    • Scope. Cables (tension- or stroke-controlled, no anchors) and pressures, alone or together, on one soft body with a tip / top region; force actuators, rigid bodies, contact, viscoelasticity, prescribed motion and moving mounts are refused or not built. A stroke cable's tendon is the scenario's (stiffness) and the stroke read back assumes the ordinary backend's model, tension = k (L - L0 + s) (an explicit follower force there; the QP has the tension as a constraint force).
    • One equilibrium. As above: the QP follows one branch from the rest state; a snapping structure may have others.
    • Bounds on the forces (tensions, pressures) and, for stroke cables, on the stroke through the iteration above; maxDispVariation / CableActuator's own displacement limits are not used (they would model a rigid tendon, not the series spring).
