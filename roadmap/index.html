    Roadmap¶

    M1: core + MuJoCo drop test (done)¶

    Config and units, materials and presets, soft/rigid geometry, tetrahedral meshing, backend-neutral scene, capability system, MuJoCo flex backend, SOFA stub (replaced by the real backend in M4), physics checks, drop-test scenario with all five dataset weights, runner, sweeps, CLI, tests (including an analytic uniaxial check, convergence and no-penetration checks), and docs.

    M2: ROS 2 layer, Humble (done; see ros2.md)¶

    • ros2/softsim_interfaces (ament_cmake): SoftBodyState, ContactWrench(Stamped), ScenarioMetrics, and services Reset, SetMaterial (preset name or YAML), RunAction (action name + YAML kwargs; drop = mass/height/preset).
    • ros2/softsim_ros (ament_python): a generic softsim_node that loads any scenario through Simulation (scenario:=… config:=… overrides:=[…]) and publishes /clock (simulated time; the simulation is ~0.005× real time), TF and pose/twist for rigid bodies, PointCloud2 and Marker mesh for soft bodies, metrics, contact wrenches and ground reaction.
    • ros2/softsim_bringup: launch with viewer on/off, headless batch mode, RViz config.

    M3: tracking + calibration (done; see experiments.md)¶

    • Dataset registry (datasets/*.yaml: DOI, files, checksums, frame rate, overlay layout) and a loader that reads TIFFs straight from the zips.
    • Tracking: green-key background, per-weight appearance (brass vs black), translucent cylinder, base flash masked. Outputs weight top-edge/knob trajectory, cylinder top and bulge width; px→mm from the 40 mm diameter; time from the frame index (2999.3 fps), checked against the overlay.
    • Calibration: joint fit of (E, beta[, contact timeconst, friction]) across all five weights with scipy; coarse-to-fine mesh; per-case RMSE; fitted preset saved with mesh and backend provenance. Plots: sim vs experiment overlays, peak compression vs mass, rebound, settling, lift-off.

    M4: SOFA backend (done; see backends/sofa.md)¶

    • SOFA v26.06 backend through SofaPython3, found via $SOFA_ROOT: linear, StVK, classic and stable Neo-Hookean, Mooney-Rivlin, one-term Ogden, Rayleigh/Kelvin-Voigt damping, rigid bodies, frictional Lagrange contact, bonded bases. Material mappings checked against analytic uniaxial compression; SOFA tests skip without SOFA.
    • stable_neo_hookean split from neo_hookean: MuJoCo's flex model is the stable variant and now says so; MuJoCo refuses classic Neo-Hookean.
    • Calibration hardening: blow-up guard in the SOFA backend (it segfaults instead of producing NaN), crash-tolerant and resumable calibrations, ground-penetration metric.
    • Results (experiments.md): Ogden fits 20/50 g within 3–11% but no material law fits 500 g; it prefers less large-strain stiffening than Neo-Hookean. Compressibility (ν = 0.45) explains part of the 500 g gap; rate dependence and the table are the remaining suspects.

    Direction¶

    softsim aims to be the soft-robotics package where every model is validated against real data, independent of the physics engine. It grows in three stages: validated sim-to-real (M1–M7, with soft materials first in M5), then design and control of real soft robots (M7–M11), then GPU learning (M12). Every robot family is planned (pneumatic, cable, gripper, magnetic). Every feature ships with a capability flag, a refusal where unsupported, an analytic test and a published-data validation.

    Drop test after the first contact (done, between M4 and M5)¶

    See experiments.md.

    • Recordings re-read: the specimen hops and drags the cloth up; it is not the table moving (M3 was wrong). The first 100 ms are now usable data.
    • Contact adhesion (material.adhesion, ground.adhesion): a cohesive law, backend-neutral (softsim/contact/adhesion.py), on MuJoCo and SOFA, with an explicit-stiffness check, verified against analytic equilibria on both backends.
    • Spring-mounted rigid bodies (mount: slider_z, MuJoCo) and the drop test's compliant support.
    • Calibration over any observable and window (weight-relative position, specimen lift), not only the first contact.
    • Fixes in the SOFA backend: light rigid bodies (CG → direct solve) and pinned bodies on the ground (NaN).
    • Reference FEM (softsim.fem, reference_fem.md): axisymmetric P2/P0, locking-free, JAX tangents, Prony viscoelasticity, punch and ground contact, verified against analytic solutions. Its 50 g drop disagrees with MuJoCo by 40% (open).

    M5: soft-material fidelity (in progress)¶

    Goal: a physical material card that runs with the same physics on every backend, with stated error. Every value can be changed from YAML, CLI, Python or ROS. The cards rest on public material-test data. Acceptance test: all five drop weights. See materials.md.

    Done:

    • Material cards:
    • kind: physical | effective, validity, uncertainty, references;
    • any two isotropic constants as input; poisson_ratio for the bulk-modulus laws;
    • new laws Gent and Arruda–Boyce;
    • new units (1/s, J/m², Pa·s, K/°C, deg/s).
    • Parameterisation:
    • softsim params lists every changeable value with its unit and range;
    • softsim material show;
    • load_material(overrides=...), Material.with_overrides, sample() from the uncertainty;
    • Simulation.set_material(body, None, overrides) and the same overrides on the ROS set_material service.
    • Homogeneous tests (softsim.materials.homogeneous): uniaxial, equibiaxial, planar, simple shear, volumetric and confined. Also relaxation, constant-rate loading and DMA with Prony. All checked against closed forms, plus softsim material plot.
    • Public data and fitting:
    • the Marechal et al. 2021 tension data (ODbL; fetched, not redistributed);
    • softsim fit-material over 7 laws, with AIC/BIC, uncertainties and a stability check;
    • softsim material project for backends that lack a law; the backends' refusal messages point to it;
    • a generated library of 18 physical cards (material_library.md).
    • Prony viscoelasticity on MuJoCo and SOFA: the long-term law is native, and the Maxwell overstress is applied as explicit vertex forces with the reference's update (exact against the homogeneous reference). It comes with an explicit-stability check.
    • Drop validation over all five weights (tools/validate_drop_bounces.py). 20 and 50 g are reproduced. 100 and 200 g are tilted edge impacts. 500 g is stiffer and lossier than any model.
    • In-plane tilt at contact from the weights' side edges (softsim.experiments.video.tilt; reliable for the dark weights). It is applied in every simulation of a recording, and it brings 500 g's specimen hop to within 10%.
    • 3D locking-free reference (softsim.fem.solid3d, 10-node tetrahedra, P2/P0, 6-DOF rigid bodies, sticking, Coulomb and adhesive contact, Prony inside the implicit solve, PARDISO) and the fem backend. It is checked against homogeneous solutions, and against the axisymmetric solver within 4% on a coarse mesh.
    • A physical Ecoflex 00-10 card (Neo-Hookean 143 kPa + Prony 0.69 at 2 ms) reproduces the 20 and 50 g first contacts on the 3D reference within 7% and 10%, with each recording's offset and spin.
    • Transfer: reference runs saved as tracks; MuJoCo calibrated against them (3–10% error bar), SOFA likewise (3–7%, calibrations/transfer_ecoflex_00_10_sofa.yaml).
    • Second dataset: Roels et al. 2025 (CC-BY-4.0), tension, compression and 3 h relaxation for 7 silicones. Their cards are fitted to tension and compression together, with Prony from the relaxation, and are now the preferred ones (25 cards in the library). fit-material handles several specimens and test kinds.
    • Ogden in 3D through matrix functions (slow; project to Yeoh or Gent for long runs).
    • Joint fits: calibration files take material_tests terms (datasets or inline datasheet points) next to simulated cases. Prepared: calibrations/ecoflex_00_10_drop_fem_joint.yaml, the drop plus the datasheet's 100% modulus. The drop-only card relaxes to about half the datasheet's quasi-static stiffness.
    • Sensitivity and uncertainty: softsim sensitivity (Morris screening) and softsim sweep --sample N from a card's uncertainty.
    • Contact fidelity, measured against the reference with elastic cards (tools/contact_restitution.py):
    • MuJoCo's flex elasticity is damped like backward Euler (ζ ≈ ω·dt/2);
    • its contact settings don't change impact losses;
    • without friction it is within 3.5% at meshes of 3.5 mm or finer, while soft-constraint friction slip costs about twice as much as everything else; contact.cone / contact.impratio mitigate part of it (backends/mujoco.md);
    • SOFA loses less with sticking friction, but much more frictionless.
    • 500 g:
    • out-of-plane tilt ruled out (a 20° sweep closes 17% of a 2× gap);
    • (since withdrawn: the two weights do not load the specimen alike, docs/experiments.md) a model-free check shows no passive specimen can explain it: the recording needs 12× the 50 g force at the same indentation and rate.
    • 3D solver ~1.8× faster (fixed sparsity pattern, PARDISO analysis once, closed-form tangents); tools/bench_fem.py for the GPU machine.

    Next:

    • Long 3D fits on the faster machine (new_machine.md): the drop-only and the joint physical card, then new reference tracks and transfers.
    • Whole bounce in 3D (tack + support) for 20 and 50 g.
    • SOFA contact fidelity: the "58% loss" frictionless was a friction mismatch (SOFA uses one coefficient for every pair); with every pair frictionless it loses 3.4% more than the reference and peaks 14% shallow (2026-11-11, backends/sofa.md). Both open artefacts are closed (2026-11-12): the rim flare went 2.83 mm through the ground because a vertex moved 0.6 mm per step against a 0.4 mm alarm distance (the backend's alarm distance now follows the per-step travel: 0.006 mm), and the "7% stiff frictionless plate" was friction (a coefficient of about 0.1 anywhere in the scene; at 0 the plate agrees with no contact to 0.6%).
    • Rate data below 1 s (DMA or impact tests) for silicones. Today's relaxation data start at 1 s, while impacts relax within milliseconds.
    • 500 g: with the material excluded, check the setup and the measurement (a second camera view, or the original authors' notes).

    M6: foundations for robots (in progress)¶

    Done so far (actuation.md): - Named surface regions on every soft shape, resolved on the mesh; fixed regions pin vertices. - Hollow shapes: hollow_sphere and closed_tube (balloon actuator), gmsh. - Pressure actuators on all three backends: exact -p V follower load in the reference; explicit follower forces on MuJoCo and SOFA. Schedules in YAML, set_control from Python, ROS 2 command and state topics. - inflation scenario (pressure–volume curve and extent). - Check: the reference matches the exact finite-strain thick-sphere solution within 0.1% up to 62% hoop strain, and it finds the limit pressure. - Backend error bar: at the same card, MuJoCo and SOFA agree with each other but inflate 27% less than the reference at 3 mm (13% at 2 mm): linear-tet locking at ν = 0.45. - softsim transfer (M5.6): fits a fast backend's effective card against the reference and checks it on unseen load cases. For MuJoCo at 3 mm, E 30 → 23.7 kPa takes the inflation error from 20% to 0.5%, and it holds at 1.5 and 4.5 kPa (1.2%, 2.2%). SOFA needs the same correction (23.6 kPa). - Bending actuator (off-axis cavity, scenarios/inflation/bending.yaml) with tip and bend-angle outputs. MuJoCo at 2 mm bends 37% less than the reference. After transfer, the error is 4% at the fitted pressure and 8% at half of it. - Cable actuators on all three backends: tendons through via points embedded in the elements, potential T L (implicit in the reference, explicit forces with a stability check on MuJoCo/SOFA), YAML/Python/ROS control. - Check: the cable-bent cantilever. The reference matches Euler–Bernoulli within 0.3% up to 0.27 rad, with the Neo-Hookean tangent under the cable's pre-compression. - Multi-material bodies (body.zones: elements with their own material, e.g. a strain-limiting layer): any law in the reference, per-zone force fields on SOFA, per-element material data on MuJoCo. Check: a two-material cable beam against composite Euler–Bernoulli (reference within 1.4% at small load). - PneuNet finger (pneunet shape: chambers, channel, conforming strain-limiting layer). MuJoCo and SOFA agree to 0.1% and bend 24% less than the reference at 8 kPa. A two-card transfer (body and layer) brings MuJoCo to 2.9%, holding at 11 kPa (3.2%) and on a 4-chamber finger (4.3%). - Bodies from files (mesh_file: STEP/IGES/BREP, STL/OBJ, .msh/VTK/Medit, .geo through gmsh). Physical groups become regions and zones, closed inner shells become cavity, and boxes name faces. Several CAD solids are bonded into one body. softsim export --cad writes the parametric shapes as STEP. The PneuNet read back from its own STEP is the same body (volumes exact) and bends within 0.5% on MuJoCo. - Zones with their own density on all backends. Check: a cantilever with a heavy outer half sags as Euler–Bernoulli says (reference within 0.15%). - Stroke-controlled cables (control: stroke: a servo reeling in a tendon of given stiffness, slack when short; implicit in the reference, explicit with a stability check on MuJoCo/SOFA). Check: the reference within 0.8% of Euler–Bernoulli with the beam's bending and axial compliance in series with the tendon. - Region forces (type: force: a dead load spread over a surface region by area, consistent on the reference's quadratic faces). Check: a tip-loaded cantilever converges to beam theory. - Moving mount (body.mount_path: the fixed regions follow a minimum-jerk translation), simulated in the mount's frame on every backend. Check: the reference agrees with a world-frame run with prescribed base motion to 0.03% (second order in the step). - Prescribed region motion (body.prescribed: any surface region follows a minimum-jerk path per axis, so a roller or a grip; the reaction force is an output on every backend). Check: a roller tension test. The reference gives the exact homogeneous stress (1e-7 at 10%, 1e-5 at 30% stretch), and force control with the reported reaction stretches the bar by the prescribed amount on all three backends; a fast pull checks the reaction against the body's momentum. - Rotating mount (body.mount_rotation: the fixed regions turn about an axis, minimum-jerk waypoints, composing with the translation). MuJoCo and SOFA simulate in the mount's frame with rotated gravity and the Euler, centrifugal and Coriolis forces; the reference runs in the world with the base nodes driven along the rotation (trapezoidal rule). Checks: the terms against numerical differentiation of a free particle, the applied forces against m a, the rotating rod's centrifugal stretch (reference +2.8%, MuJoCo -1.6%, SOFA -2.3%), the Euler lag at the start, and a spinning round beam that must move as a still one (reference 6.4%; the linear-tet backends 17-21%, from the mesh's anisotropy and implicit Euler, with each broken force 48-355%). - Cable anchor spread over a face (anchor: <region>: the end force becomes the linear traction with the same resultant and moment, on all backends). Check: the reference is within 0.5% of Euler-Bernoulli at the tip (a point anchor 2.6% low), and under stroke control within 0.7% with no stiff cap (a point anchor -19%). - Zones with their own damping (reference only: a Prony series, or none, per zone; MuJoCo and SOFA have one damping per body and refuse). Check: the plate force of a body with a quarter-section zone of another series is the area-weighted sum of each series' exact history. - Cable finger (cable_finger shape: segments cut by slots, a tendon through the web, path: geometry). Check: exact mesh volume, one segment is Euler-Bernoulli (-0.9%), four segments bend 2.54 times as far, mesh converged to 0.5%; a plane-section beam model (1.55 times) is a lower bound. - Analytic checks: the closed-end thick tube (Lame, free extension) on the reference converges at second order (radial -10% to -0.5% from 4 to 1.3 mm; axial strain +0.3% at 1.8 mm); SOFA's Rayleigh alpha against a free fall with linear drag (the implicit-Euler recurrence to 1e-12). - Closed-loop control from Python: Simulation.run(controller=...) (a PI loop holding a cable beam's tip angle is a test).

    Still to do: - ~~More actuators: a constant-rate rotating mount~~ done 2026-11-11: waypoints [time, angle, rate] (actuation.md). - Backend mappings (optional, for speed or stability): tried and not shipped. SoftRobots' CableConstraint (plugin present in the SOFA v26.06 install; load it with RequiredPlugin SoftRobots, via points as a BarycentricMapping child, pullPoint a little behind the first point) in valueType="force" reproduces the explicit cable exactly: the cable cantilever's tip angle at 0.7 N (a step, 2 mm, 1 ms) agreed to 1e-14 at steps 50, 100, 200 and 500 (0.2837, 0.1523, 0.2471, 0.2861 rad). The solver's λ is an impulse, so the value must be force × dt (a force of 0.01 N without the factor gave 6 m/s). A force-type constraint is still an applied tension at the step's start, so it has no stability advantage over ConstantForceField; the one that would is the displacement type (a rigid tendon, stiffness → ∞), which is a different model from the finite-stiffness tendon here, and SOFA-only. Not pursued: it needs a rigid-tendon option in the config first. SurfacePressureConstraint was not tried. MuJoCo spatial tendons attach to sites on bodies, not to barycentric points inside flex elements, so the cable's via points would have to be vertices.

    M7: pneumatic finger (in progress; blocked on a data decision)¶

    PneuNet scenario (pressure → bending angle, tip force, cavity volume), public datasets registered with DOI and licence, and a generic experiment API so calibration and plots work for any scenario. Yeoh in SOFA (custom force field) or refused.

    Done: - PneuNet scenario, backends compared and MuJoCo transferred (above). - Generic experiment API: softsim fit (any observable column of any scenario against a registered measured table, curve or series; docs/experiments.md). Checked by a twin experiment: it recovers a hidden modulus to 0.4% (clean) and 3.5% (1% noise); four code mutants are caught. - Yeoh in SOFA: refused, with the softsim material project hint. - Finer meshes on the GPU: the element kernels run in chunks (a 100k-element launch asked the 12 GB card for 4.8 GB and failed); the reference PneuNet now runs to 0.7 mm (152k tetrahedra, 25 min for four loads) and converges to within about 0.4% per step; the 1.5 mm reference is 3.6% under-bent (docs/actuation.md).

    Open: real data. No public pressure–angle dataset with a geometry and material I can read was usable; the options and my recommendation are in decisions.md, entry 3.

    M8: cable finger, soft arm and rods¶

    Done on the reference: the cable-finger shape and scenario (cable_finger, docs/actuation.md) and a two-segment cable arm with stiff disks (cable_arm.yaml, within 2% of piecewise Euler–Bernoulli). Not done: a rod (Cosserat) backend for slender bodies (SOFA BeamAdapter, PyElastica or our own): chosen by the recommendation, SOFA BeamAdapter (decisions.md, entry 4). Done: --backend rod (backends/rod.md): a slender clamped body of any scenario is reduced to beam frames (section stiffnesses from the tets with material zones exact; the mesh follows the frames), cables and region forces carried by virtual work. Verified on the tip load (0.8641 mm vs E–B 0.864), cable cantilever, 64° large rotation (arc of the compressed beam), layered section and the S of the two-segment arm (linear E–B within 0.7%; the 3D reference is 8% lower from the compression stiffening 1 + 2N/EA, which a materially linear rod cannot have); 16 tests, 6 mutants caught, a figure. Open: alpha damping is inert on BeamAdapter's mass (refused), the explicit cable step limit (the rod backend's; the SOFA FEM cable is now implicit), and rods that are not columns along z (curved or branched), PneuNet-like bodies being out of scope.

    M9: soft gripper and grasping¶

    N-finger gripper on a rigid palm. Grasp metrics (success, contact patch, forces, slip, pull-out). Contact quality work, including the M4 SOFA artefacts, finger self-collision (done for MuJoCo and SOFA, one geometry) and a friction benchmark. A grasp benchmark grid.

    Started 2026-11-06: two-finger gripper on the reference, a fast finger fitted to it, a local live page and a grasp map (gripper.md). Two finite-element grasps with Coulomb friction (10 g held, 30 g dropped) agree with the fast finger and play in the page's Truth tab. The finite-element finger's tip force is about 5.5 times below the measurement with its tip sliding on the gauge, and about twice above with its tip stuck to it: the unrecorded contact explains the gap, so the force cannot test the model (2026-11-11, actuation.md).

    2026-11-11: friction benchmark (scenarios/actuation/sliding_block.yaml, benchmarks/sliding_block.yaml and sticking_block.yaml, tests/test_friction.py). A silicone block pushed across the ground against exact Coulomb sliding: all three backends within 0.8-0.94%. Below the limit, MuJoCo creeps at 0.13 mm/s (soft constraints) and the reference at a rate set by its time step (its friction smoothing is per step; decisions.md, entry 6).

    2026-11-11: self-collision (body.self_collision, tests/test_self_collision.py). A finger pulled until its slot closes: with self-collision on, the slot's walls stay apart (MuJoCo +0.19 mm, SOFA +0.10 mm); off, they pass through by 1.3 mm. fem and rod refuse it. Verified on one geometry only (actuation.md).

    M10: magnetic soft robots (MRE)¶

    Soft-magnetic elastomer first, because that is what isotropic MRE fingers are: carbonyl-iron particles with no remanence, magnetised by the applied field. The energy is linear-magnetic with axes that follow the deformation, in a given field (uniform plus a point dipole); the field is not solved for. Then a permanent magnet's field, an MRE finger against a measured force, and a magnetic gripper. A hard-magnetic model (fixed magnetisation) follows if a design needs it.

    Started 2026-11-08: softsim/fem/magnetic.py on the reference, checked against a turned body's energy, the pull up a dipole's gradient and a cantilever's closed form up to buckling (tests/test_magnetic.py).

    2026-11-09 (magnetic.md):

    • Config. magneto_active cards take a susceptibility or an iron fraction, with block demagnetising factors. A scene-level magnetic_field: holds a uniform field and ring or disc magnets, which can move along paths and can be solid.
    • Field. The magnets' fields are exact, checked against current loops to 1e-7.
    • Checks. A bar built from the config follows its closed form to 2.2%. A mutation sweep tries 32 slips.
    • Against Bernat et al.'s gripper (arXiv:2407.13477), digitised from the paper's vector figures:
    • The rolling torque is flat, as in their ANSYS. The point where it falls to zero suggests a strip about 2 mm thick.
    • With the susceptibility from the recipe alone, the torque is 58-66% of theirs.
    • The 0.7 N finger force is not checked yet: the paper does not give the contact point.
    • Solid magnets. A magnet can also be a rigid cylinder for contact. Each magnet has a chamfer (edge, 0.2 mm) that softens its field's singular rims and rounds its contact surface: the singular rims had drawn a strip into the magnet's corner.
    • Fixed: H inside a magnet. It is now B / mu0 - M; taking B / mu0 made a false well under the rim.
    • Finger force.
    • Energy balance for a free end peeled along the rim's tangent gives 0.24-0.28 N on our torque and 0.44 N on ANSYS's, against 0.66-0.83 N measured.
    • A clamped strip pulled that way cannot peel. Finite elements show the force rising past 1.9 N.
    • So the measured force depends on the gauge's unrecorded contact point.
    • Not yet simulated:
    • the 15 mm strip, whose overhanging flaps snap onto the magnet's poles;
    • the strip closing from a gap: it hits the magnet at metres per second.
    • Next:
    • ~~saturation~~ done 2026-11-11: a tanh law from the card's saturation_magnetization (0 keeps the linear law), checked against the closed forms (magnetic.md);
    • the finger against a gauge, once the set-up is known;
    • ~~a hard-magnetic model~~ done 2026-11-11: remanent magnetisation -mu0 (F M_r) . H (Zhao et al. 2019), checked against closed forms (magnetic.md).
    • ~~magnetic zones~~ done 2026-11-11: zones carry their own magneto_active card (hard, soft or none), the energy assembled per element; two zones of opposite remanence cancel and a non-magnetic one halves the energy, to 1e-12; a zone with induced magnetisation needs explicit demagnetising factors (block is refused);
    • ~~a hard-magnetic gripper on the reference~~ done 2026-11-11: a U-shaped body with two oppositely magnetised arms closes in a uniform field (scenarios/magnetic/hard_magnetic_gripper.yaml); the arm's bending is the cantilever's under a distributed couple to 1.4% at 1 mm (0.5% at 0.75 mm), the tip 17-18% more because the palm rotates at the root. Not yet: gripping an object (a GPU-machine job, magnetic.md).

    M11: design and control toolkit¶

    Quasi-static inverse control (done: softsim inverse by repeated equilibrium solves, and the SoftRobots.Inverse fast path on SOFA, --method sofa-qp; see control.md), reduced-order surrogates for real-time ROS 2 control (done: quasi-static polynomial/RBF maps and a linear DMD model, softsim surrogate; closed-loop Controller with surrogate feedforward, integral feedback and an MPC-lite option, surrogate.md; Koopman not done), design optimisation over parametric geometry (softsim optimize), and sim-to-real identification from short recorded motions.

    • Design optimisation (done): softsim optimize <job.yaml> (design.md). Nelder-Mead over dotted design parameters (units, bounds, log, integer, tied keys), objective maximise/minimise/target on any summary metric (or a ratio), constraints as penalties with the best chosen among feasible evaluations and infeasibility reported, resumable evaluation log, parallel initial simplex. Checked on the cable cantilever against a known maximum (the bound) and a bisection reference; the MuJoCo-locking bias of the optimum and the fem check are documented. Open in M11: sim-to-real identification.
    • SOFA fast path for inverse control (done): softsim/control/sofa_inverse.py, softsim inverse --method sofa-qp (control.md). The SoftRobots.Inverse plugin loads and works in the v26.06 binary (QPInverseProblemSolver, CableActuator, PositionEffector). The scene is built beside the SOFA backend, not inside it (no backend change). Cantilever tension within 0.6-1.2% of the repeated solve on the same backend and 7-11 times faster (1 s); the two-cable arm in 2.8 s where the repeated solve on SOFA does not settle (336 s, stalled, with the then explicit cable force; see the next bullet); a target beyond the bounds gives the bounded solution, flagged.
